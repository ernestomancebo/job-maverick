# Canadian resume rules (what recruiters and ATS expect)

Source: career-services guidance from BC universities, BC pay-transparency practice, and what worked in 2026 applications.

## Format
- 2 pages maximum for experienced candidates; 1 page for entry level. The master resume is exempt (it's a source, not a submission).
- Font 11pt or larger; margins 0.5" or more; one simple Word layout saved to PDF. No tables, text boxes, columns, headers/footers, or icons: ATS parsers drop them.
- No photo, age, date of birth, marital status, religion, SIN, or hobbies. Pronouns optional.
- Header: city and province, phone, professional email, LinkedIn; GitHub for engineers. A work-authorization line ("Open Work Permit, no sponsorship required"; "Permanent Resident"; "Citizen") saves a screening question.
- Put the job title you're applying for, and the requisition number if the posting has one, directly under the header.
- ATS-safe characters only: write "from 0% to 94%", not "0% → 94%"; "cut 50%", not "−50%"; "about 420", not "~420".
- File name: `<Full Name> Resume – <Role> – <Company>.pdf`. Cover letter, if asked, as the last page of the same PDF.

## Content
- Summary: 3-5 sentences, factual, no "eager to apply my skills at X".
- Highlights of Qualifications: 4-6 statements that mirror the posting's keywords, each with evidence. This block is what the 6-10 second scan reads.
- Experience: reverse chronological; focus on the last 10 years; 3-5 achievement statements per role; action verb + task + quantified result; present tense for the current role's ongoing duties, past tense for completed work.
- Titles must match what a background check will find. For contract work, name the employer of record ("Verizon, via <agency>").
- Education after experience for anyone past entry level. Note a credential evaluation (WES, ICAS) for foreign degrees.
- Volunteering counts as community involvement and as Canadian experience; keep it factual (role, organization, dates, one line).
- References: not on the resume unless the posting asks.

## Signals that matter in Canada
- Collaboration and influence stories, not only metrics ("how I brought the team along").
- Consensus-driven, less hierarchical leadership language.
- Local presence: a provincial address, a provincial employer, local volunteering or meetups.
