# Market: Canada

Applies to any profession. Source: career-services guidance from Canadian universities, provincial pay-transparency practice, and what worked in 2026 applications.

## Resume format
- 2 pages maximum for experienced candidates; 1 page for entry level. The master resume is exempt (it's a source, not a submission).
- Font 11pt or larger; margins 0.5" or more; one simple Word layout saved to PDF. No tables, text boxes, columns, headers/footers, or icons: ATS parsers drop them.
- No photo, age, date of birth, marital status, religion, SIN, or hobbies. Pronouns optional.
- Header: city and province, phone, professional email, LinkedIn; a portfolio link where the profession has one. A work-authorization line ("Open Work Permit, no sponsorship required"; "Permanent Resident"; "Citizen") saves a screening question.
- Put the job title you're applying for, and the requisition number if the posting has one, directly under the header.
- ATS-safe characters only: "from 0% to 94%", not "0% → 94%"; "cut 50%", not "−50%"; "about 420", not "~420".
- File name: `<Full Name> Resume – <Role> – <Employer>.pdf`. Cover letter, if asked, as the last page of the same PDF.

## Content
- Summary: 3-5 sentences, factual.
- Highlights of Qualifications: 4-6 statements that mirror the posting's keywords, each with evidence. This block is what the 6-10 second scan reads.
- Experience: reverse chronological; focus on the last 10 years; 3-5 achievement statements per role; action verb + task + quantified result.
- Titles must match what a background check will find. For contract or agency work, name the employer of record.
- Education after experience for anyone past entry level. Note a credential evaluation (WES, ICAS, IQAS) for foreign degrees.
- Volunteering counts as community involvement and as Canadian experience; keep it factual.
- References: not on the resume unless the posting asks.

## Signals that matter here
- Collaboration and influence stories, not only metrics ("how I brought the team along").
- Consensus-driven, less hierarchical leadership language.
- Local presence: a provincial address, a provincial employer, local volunteering or associations.

## Pay transparency and negotiation
- BC: every public posting must show a salary or range (Pay Transparency Act, 2023); salary-history questions are banned. Ontario: posted ranges required from 2026. Use posted ranges as the primary comp source.
- Benefits that matter: RRSP/DPSP match 3-6%, extended health/dental/vision, vacation 3-4 weeks standard (more at senior levels), parental top-up. Stock options at a CCPC get favourable tax treatment.

## Immigration and work authorization
- Open work permits: say so in the header. Employer-specific permits: targeting is constrained; say so in `profile.md`.
- Provincial nominee programs (e.g., BC PNP) usually need a job offer from an employer **in that province**; a remote role for an out-of-province employer may not qualify. A written employer commitment to support the nomination is worth negotiating for.
- Express Entry (CEC) needs skilled Canadian work experience; plan the first role to start that clock.

## Regulated professions: credential path before applying
- **Nurses:** NNAS assessment, then registration with the provincial college (e.g., BCCNM in BC); language test; possible bridging. Employers won't interview without active or provisional registration.
- **Accountants:** CPA Canada assessment of foreign designations (many have MRAs: ACCA, CPA US, CA ANZ, ICAI varies); "CPA candidate" is a legitimate resume line.
- **Teachers:** provincial teacher certification (e.g., BC Teachers' Council); evaluation of foreign credentials can take months.
- **Engineers (licensed work):** provincial association (e.g., EGBC); many software roles don't need it, but "P.Eng" matters in civil/mechanical/electrical.
- **Building/facilities managers:** no licence for the role; trades, boiler, elevator and fire-safety certifications are often required for the team; BOMA (FMA/RPA), IREM (CPM), IFMA (FMP/CFM) credentials matter.
